# Audit, 2026-10-25

A read-only audit of the web product (UI and UX) and of the prediction model and pipeline. Nothing was changed. Evidence: the Impeccable design detector on every built page, axe-core 4.10 in a real browser on every page, mobile and desktop measurements in the browser pane, an ECC machine-learning review of the code (`ecc:mle-reviewer`), and the live record of 145 matches settled between 9 and 22 October 2026 (209,000 settled selections, read from the production database).

## Part 1. Prediction model and pipeline

### Verdict

The model is doing what its backtests said it would. It is **not** a bookmaker-beating model (our own tests say so, and the site says so), but its probabilities are calibrated and it beats the usual frequencies in every family. There is one clear live regression (the lineup-aware player prices), and the "stored before kickoff, never edited" promise is enforced by convention more than by the database.

### What the live record shows (145 matches, 9 to 22 October)

| Check | Result |

These samples are small (62 matches for the main model). Read them as "no sign of trouble", not as proof.

### Findings, ranked

**Critical / High**

1. **The lineup-aware player prices (`players-0.4.0`) are badly wrong, and they replace the weekly ones on the site.** On the 38 matches that have both runs, the lineup version predicted 22% on average where 31% happened, and its log loss is worse on every market (anytime goal 0.49 against 0.32, shots 0.63 against 0.50, fouls 0.64 against 0.49). Cause: the weekly rate already contains how often a player is available, and the lineup step keeps a starter's share unchanged rather than raising it to "he is starting". Every number for a named starter is therefore too low (calibration by band: players priced at 3.6% happened 8.6% of the time, at 7.3% happened 13.8%, at 27% happened 38%). The weekly run (`0.3.0`) over-predicts only the top bands (57% against 52%). **Action:** stop showing the lineup version (export should prefer `0.3.0`) until the adjustment is re-fitted and validated on stored lineups.
2. **"Stored before kickoff" is not enforced by the database** (reviewer, `002_predictions.sql`, `weekly.py`). The triggers stop updates and deletes, but nothing rejects an insert after kickoff, a run's time stamp is the start of its transaction (before the model fit), `--force` can create a second official run, and the confidence tier is recomputed at export time from the current rules, so rebuilding the rules relabels history. **Action:** an insert trigger that rejects rows once `clock_timestamp()` passes kickoff, a unique index per match, version and run type, and the tier and rules hash written at insert.
3. **The confidence rules overlap the live window and `docs/model-card.md` is stale** (reviewer). The rules file records a backtest to 2026-10-14 (8,383 matches) while the model card says 1,400 matches to February; the held-out table describes different rules from the ones shipped; the match count mixes ID schemes. Live tier checks above are partly in-sample for families F to I. **Action:** rebuild per family with a hard cut before 2026-10-08 and rewrite the model card.
4. **Player markets are rated High against a trivial baseline** (reviewer). Every player is the selection "player", so the baseline is one flat chance for all players and any model that separates stars from the rest looks skilful. The tuning rows feed the tier data, production prices a different player set from the backtest, and a player who did not play is settled as lost (bookmakers void it) although the log knows whether he played. **Action:** a per-player or matchup baseline, cap family I at Medium until then, and void did-not-play bets.
5. **The lineup job can lose a re-price and can zero real starters** (reviewer). The lineup rows are committed before the pricing runs, so a failure leaves a match with a lineup and no 0.4.0 run, never retried. The fuzzy name match scores ESPN "Gabriel" against Understat "Gabriel Magalhães" at about 0.58, which zeroes the starter and boosts the rest by up to 35%, with no check that most starters matched. It also selects matches that have already kicked off.
6. **European cups, live but unvalidated** (reviewer): ESPN box-score statistics for matches that went to extra time cover 120 minutes but feed the corner, card and shot models; counts, card timing and players run for cups although only the goals model was backtested; a failed club match silently creates a new club (rated as an unseen average club) with no alert. The season of a fixture (ESPN year) and of its result (kickoff) could disagree, which would split one match in two.

**Medium**

7. The Championship has negative live skill (-4.7%, 13 matches). It uses the goals-model settings, half-time table and timing profile of the five rated leagues. Watch it; do not claim skill there.
8. Count models assume the two teams' counts are independent (cards, corners, shots totals share referee and tempo effects). Player-carded probabilities above 20% were over-confident (22% predicted, 15% happened).
9. The half-time split and goal-timing profile come from five leagues and Understat minutes, and are applied to the Championship and cups, whose minutes come from ESPN.
10. Ten adoption decisions were judged on the same 2022 to 2026 holdout, and the adopted changes moved log loss by 0.0006 to 0.0007, inside noise. The half-life and ridge come from one league's 2019 to 2021 data.
11. One failing league step skips the export and deploy for every league (the CI steps have no `if: always()`); an unmapped club stops that league's fixtures for the day.
12. Re-settlement only triggers on a changed full-time score; corrections to half-time score, corners or cards never re-settle, and voids are re-settled on every run.

**Low:** quarter-line half-win and half-loss settle as full results (dormant, no quarter lines priced); `data_cutoff` records midnight, not the real data cut; the same version id can hold changed parameters without a record; two same-named players on one squad are merged.

### Three changes most worth making, in order

1. Fix or hide the lineup-aware player prices (finding 1). It is live, visible and measurably wrong.
2. Enforce the integrity promise in the database (finding 2). Everything the product sells rests on it.
3. Rebuild the confidence tiers with a hard date cut and a fair player baseline (findings 3 and 4), then rewrite the model card.

### What was checked and is sound

The Dixon-Coles and cross-league fits use only matches before the date; pricing and settlement share one function; goal-minute markets are settled only when the minutes were verified; own goals are excluded from player markets; the kickoff void exists; tuning for the goals model was done on 2019 to 2021 and run once on 2022 onward.

The reviewer's own test run later finished: 53 passed (settlement, weekly, confidence, lineups and migration tests); the full suite passed 478 tests when last run in full. Not verified: live database privileges (whether the owner role can drop the triggers), ESPN's season convention for cups, whether Understat and ESPN place a goal in the same minute.

## Part 2. UI and UX

Method: Impeccable audit (detector on 10 built pages, five-dimension scoring), axe-core on `/`, `/app`, a match page, results, a result page, the track record, about and privacy, and measurements on a 375 px phone viewport. Admin was not rendered (it needs the live database).

### Audit health score: 15 / 20 (Good)

| # | Dimension | Score | Key finding |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Accessibility | 3 | One serious axe violation: a scrollable table on the track record cannot be reached with the keyboard |
| 2 | Performance | 3 | Every app page inlines the 116 KB fixtures index; the results page is 277 KB of HTML and 3,300 elements |
| 3 | Responsive | 3 | No horizontal overflow anywhere; 11 to 60 touch targets per page are under 44 px |
| 4 | Theming | 2 | A full token system, but no dark mode and several hard-coded colours |
| 5 | Implementation integrity | 4 | Detector clean on every page; one coherent, product-specific system |

### Implementation integrity verdict: pass

The detector reports nothing on the landing page, app pages, results, track record and legal pages (it found three things on the landing page earlier, all fixed). Tokens, type (Lora italic with IBM Plex Sans) and the navy panel for the record page express one system and nothing interchangeable with another product.

### Findings

**P1 (fix before wider release)**
- **Stale and contradictory claims.** The fixtures notice says "Predictions use results only: no lineups, injuries or suspensions" and every match page repeats "Lineups ... are not used yet", although lineup-aware prices exist. `PRODUCT.md` still describes 176 markets, `goals-dc-0.2.0` and "confidence tiers are not calibrated yet". Either show honest, per-match wording (lineup used or not) or fix the lineup model first (model finding 1). Suggested: `/impeccable clarify`.
- **A scrollable table the keyboard cannot reach, on the track record.** `.panel:nth-child(6) > .tbl-wrap` scrolls horizontally but is not focusable (axe `scrollable-region-focusable`, serious; WCAG 2.1.1). Give scrollable table wrappers `tabindex="0"`, a role and a label. Suggested: `/impeccable harden`.

**P2 (next pass)**
- **Touch targets.** Navigation links are 37 px high, league pills 36 px, line chips 28 px, the back link 22 px; 18 targets on the fixtures page and 60 on a match page are under 44 px. They meet the 24 px minimum of WCAG 2.2 AA but not common touch guidance. Suggested: `/impeccable adapt`.
- **Match page is very long** (about 7,900 px on a phone with 10 market tabs). There is a good "At a glance" summary, but no way to jump to a market from it and the search box sits below the fold. Suggested: `/impeccable layout`.
- **Results page is 34,000 px long on a phone** (3,300 elements, 277 KB). Group by day with collapsible days or paginate. Suggested: `/impeccable optimize`.
- **Fixtures lists matches that have already been played** until the next daily run (in my local build, made on Sunday from the production database, the list still showed Friday's matches), and offers league filters with no matches ("Champions League 0", "Europa League 0"). Hide or disable empty filters; show "played, result pending". Suggested: `/impeccable harden`.
- **Market tabs are toggle buttons (`aria-pressed`) in a `role="group"`, not tabs.** Screen-reader users get no tab semantics or arrow-key movement. Either use the tabs pattern or a clear single-select group.
- **No team search or "today" shortcut on the fixtures page**, only league filters; the only entry to a club's matches is scrolling.
- **Perf: every `/app` page embeds the whole fixtures index** (116 KB uncompressed) in its HTML because the shared layout loads it, plus the match file; match pages are 229 KB. Load only what each page needs. Suggested: `/impeccable optimize`.
- **No dark mode** (no `prefers-color-scheme` rule anywhere) and hard-coded colours outside the tokens (the Google button, `#1448b8` chip text, white and grey values on the landing page). Suggested: `/impeccable colorize`.
- **Link previews and discovery.** There is no favicon file, no Open Graph tags (a shared link in WhatsApp shows plain text), and every page including the public landing page carries `noindex, nofollow`. Decide deliberately which public pages may be found. Suggested: `/impeccable polish`.
- **Jargon in user-facing text:** "GG/NG", "Model goals-dc-0.3.0" in the match summary, "log loss" and "skill" on the track record. Plain wording and a short glossary. Suggested: `/impeccable clarify`.
- **Cups lack context:** no stage (league phase, play-off, knockout), no first-leg result or aggregate, although the markets are for 90 minutes only.

**P3 (polish)**
- The "v" between team names touches the second name on the fixtures cards ("vBorussia").
- Google Fonts are loaded from a third party on every page (privacy line already says so); self-hosting removes the dependency and a render-blocking request.
- Feedback is asked only at the bottom of a match page; none on fixtures, results or the track record.
- First visit after sign-in has no orientation (what the bars mean, what to open first); the explanation sits in a collapsed "What do the confidence bars mean?" on the match page.

### Patterns and systemic issues

- **Documentation drifts faster than the product:** `PRODUCT.md`, `docs/model-card.md` and on-page copy each describe an earlier version.
- **Interactive controls are consistently a little small** (pills, chips, links) because one spacing scale is used for desktop and touch.
- **Heavy shared layout:** data needed by one page is loaded for all.

### What is working well

- Zero axe violations on eight of nine pages checked; a real skip link, one `h1` per page, landmarks, `lang`, visible focus rings, reduced-motion handled.
- No horizontal overflow at 375 px on any page checked.
- The honesty of the product: confidence bars, the "At a glance" most-reliable list, a public track record with misses shown, the explicit "not advice" and bookmaker-comparison notes.
- Public pages carry no prediction data in their HTML (checked after the landing-page change).

### Recommended order

1. Model: fix or hide the lineup-aware player prices (Part 1, finding 1), then update the copy (`/impeccable clarify`).
2. `/impeccable harden`: the keyboard-inaccessible table, empty and stale states on the fixtures page.
3. Model: database enforcement of kickoff and tier recording (Part 1, finding 2).
4. `/impeccable adapt` and `/impeccable optimize`: touch targets, results page length, per-page data loading.
5. `/impeccable colorize` (dark mode, tokens) and `/impeccable polish` (favicon, previews, indexing decision).
6. Model: rebuild the tiers with a date cut, then the rest of the model findings.

Re-run the audit after these fixes to see the score move.

## Status after the follow-up (2026-10-25)

Done: lineup-aware player prices paused (lineups still stored; `players-0.4.0` hidden from the site); family I (player markets) capped at Medium;
migration 011 (database refuses official predictions after kickoff and a second official run per version, backtests exempt, `--force` lifts it);
cups no longer store ESPN team statistics for matches that went beyond 90 minutes; the daily job exports and deploys even when one league fails;
a player-card or foul bet is void when the player did not play (`python -m pitchside.settle.settle --redo-player-logs` re-settles history once);
same-day results job (`results.yml`); Fixtures lists only matches not yet kicked off; Results grouped by game week, six weeks kept.
Still open: counts, card timing and players for the cups remain on as experimental (unrated) instead of off; the lineup-pricing atomicity and 9-of-11 starter check (only needed if pricing resumes); the UI items P1-P3 not touched here.

## Follow-up changes in the workspace (2026-10-30)

- Migration 013 makes the official-run check cover the whole match/model/run-type, and serializes concurrent inserts for that key. `--force` no longer disables the database guard.
- New predictions store their confidence tier; each prediction run records the SHA-256 hash of the confidence rules used. Exports prefer that stored tier, preserving labels when rules are later rebuilt.
- `scripts/build_tiers.py` now defaults to an exclusive 2026-10-08 cutoff. The refreshed rules use 8,300 matches through 2026-09-20 and their held-out split is documented in `docs/model-card.md`.
- The lineup repricing was already paused; the stale landing-page claim that lineups are used has been corrected.

These are source and artifact changes only. Migration 013 will apply through the normal pipeline migration step; this follow-up did not connect to or alter the production database.
